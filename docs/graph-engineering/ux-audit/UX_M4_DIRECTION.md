### 1.4 Workflow library (real, 1280×720)

One dialog, correctly structured in five sections, but as **one long scroll**: Workflow (select and sentence), Versions (sentence, two rows, duplicate field, Archive), Use (a banner, the run request, the whole binding form, Context, Checks), then Share and Advanced as closed triangles. To choose a version and press **Open in Runs** the user scrolls past the entire Use form; the primary action sits at the bottom, with no header or footer to anchor it.

### 1.5 Why it reads as "a black background with differently styled text and buttons" (evidence, not opinion)

- **Surfaces are one grey.** In Zai Dark the sidebar and the workspace are the **same** `#161616`; header and panel are `#202020`; cards `#2b2b2b`. The eye cannot separate navigation, main work surface and secondary panel. Zai Light separates them by only about 3% lightness (`#f0f0f0` vs `#f8f8f8`).
- **There is no accent.** The brand colour is white (dark) or black (light), so the only ways to say "this matters" are text weight, a white button, and orange boxes. Selection, focus and the primary action share one vocabulary with "warning".
- **Every group is a bordered box, and boxes nest** (card → tile → panel; list row → tag). Outlines are the main grouping device, so nothing is visually quieter than anything else.
- **Type carries five or six sizes per screen** (heading, body, caption, small, extra-small) with grey-on-grey secondary text. Computed from the token formulas (Tailwind neutral-300 ≈ `#d4d4d4`, neutral-800 ≈ `#262626`; approximate): secondary text is about 5:1 on the dark canvas but about 4.4:1 on a card and about 4:1 in Zai Light; the "subtlest" tier is about 2.2:1 in Zai Dark. Much of the explanatory copy uses these tiers, which is part of why it is hard to read and easy to ignore.
- **Every section opens with a heading plus a grey sentence.** The sentences are the largest block of repeated text.

These are observations about the current screens. They do not establish that any specific user task is slow; that belongs to the human review.

## 2. The two alternatives

Both keep the product semantics, ownership and contracts (nothing about what is authorized, frozen or approved changes; saved configuration stays separate from captured results; native permission stays separate from final approval; every blocker stays on screen at the point of decision; Advanced, the graph editor, history paging and Needs-you remain reachable). Both replace "bordered boxes on one grey" with **four distinguishable surface roles** and **one restrained accent used only for the primary action and the current selection**; warning and danger keep their own hues and are always paired with an icon and words.

## 3. Recommendation: **A — Focus page**

It answers the stated complaint most directly: the user sees the task and the next action first, each state has one focal element, and a flat "black with differently styled text" becomes a layered surface system with a single accent. It also matches the product's own success measure (a short, low-anxiety repeat loop: write the task, glance at the setup, review).

**Principal trade-off.** A shows **less at once**. Step detail, evidence, Share and Advanced are one tab click away, and at 1920×1080 the 780 px column leaves wide empty margins (the alternative spends that width on an inspector). The cost is one extra click to reach detail that the current screen always shows; the mitigation is that everything needed to _decide_ (the blocker, the permission location, the failing cause, required fields, unsaved/version-change consequences, the acknowledgment) stays in the banner or the action bar and is never inside a tab.

**Why not B.** B is the better use of a wide monitor and keeps setup editable while reading readiness, but it keeps _more_ simultaneously visible by design, which is the opposite of the complaint, and it squeezes at 1280×720 (the center pane is about 430 px; the boards show the wrapping). If the review prefers "see everything" over "see one thing", B is a coherent alternative; a hybrid (A's page with an optional right facts column only at ≥1600 px) is possible later and is not proposed now.

## 4. What the prototype does **not** show yet (so it is not mistaken for complete)

- Review/preflight, final approval, the graph canvas and inspector, Checks editing, history paging and the keyboard/focus treatment are not drawn. M4.3 must design them with the same roles; nothing in either alternative requires removing them.
- Content that moves into tabs/links rather than disappearing: the full step list, routing inspection, captured request/result, individual checks, file changes, technical identities, digests, library revision and manual JSON.
- Real strings: the prototype's English is invented or shortened from the real copy. Consent-critical wording (permission, preflight disclosures, acknowledgment, freshness, approval scope) is **not** rewritten here and must keep its contractual text in M4.3.
- Simplified Chinese, the 125% and 150% Windows scales and non-Graph screens were not inspected; they belong to M4.4.

## 5. Proposed design brief (to replace parts of DESIGN.md **only after direction approval**)

1. **Surface roles (tokens, both themes):** `shell` (sidebar, header strip), `canvas` (main work area), `raised` (panels, rows, inputs), `overlay` (dialogs, menus). Each must be visibly different from its neighbour in both themes (today dark shell = canvas).
2. **Accent:** one restrained hue for primary action, selection and links only. Warning and danger keep their own semantic tokens and always appear with an icon and text.
3. **Type:** keep the `text-ui-*` mechanism and `--ui-font-size`. Fixed hierarchy: page title, section title (one per section), body, secondary, label. Decision needed: the prototype's 22 px page title is larger than `text-ui-xl` (18 px); either use `text-ui-xl` or add a `text-ui-2xl` step. **Secondary text must reach 4.5:1 on its actual surface**, including Zai Light and cards; the "subtlest" tier is reserved for placeholders.
4. **Grouping:** whitespace and alignment first, hairline dividers second, a surface change third, a bordered box last. No card around a paragraph; at most one panel level inside a page; no panel inside a panel.
5. **Copy:** one sentence says each thing once, at the point it matters. A section heading is followed by content, not by a sentence restating the heading. Consent-critical text is exempt from shortening.
6. **Actions:** one primary per state (accent-filled), secondary outlined, tertiary text; destructive only for destructive effects.
7. **Detail:** reached through tabs or named links, one level deep. No nested collapsibles, no mandatory disclosure, nothing that blocks a decision inside a tab.
8. **Shared-token rule:** any change to shared `--color-*` tokens is first inspected on the non-Graph consumers (Chat, plugin store, settings, login); Graph-only roles may be added without touching them.
